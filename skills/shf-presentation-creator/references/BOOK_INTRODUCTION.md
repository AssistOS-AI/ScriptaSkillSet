# A book introduction that earns the next page

Use this mode for a book-page presentation, reading invitation, introduction or
promotional companion. Its outcome is an informed desire to read the book.
It is distinct from an explanatory summary, technical demonstration or tutorial.
Choose the communication purpose before choosing a genre playbook. Identify the
genre from the source itself, not the title or neighboring books in a batch.
A cultural essay named after a mythical figure needs an argument-led invitation,
not a monster trailer; an executable-science proposal must retain its proposal
status rather than becoming a claim of experimentally established capability.

## Position the book before drafting scenes

Write `purpose: "book-introduction"` in the editorial plan and include a
`readerInvitation` object with:

- `reasonToExist`: the gap, question or tension this particular book addresses.
- `readerPromise`: the useful change in understanding that reading may provide.
- `readerOutcomes`: specific distinctions, questions or judgments the reader can learn.
- `bookJourney`: how the source develops those ideas, with chapter/section references.
- `audienceFit`: who has reason to care, and relevant limits of the book's scope.
- `contentSamples`: a few scene IDs and the reading value demonstrated by each.
- `reservedForReading`: worthwhile depth actually present in the book, with locators.
- `closingInvitation`: a concrete, truthful invitation and a sensible starting point.

Do not promise author intentions you cannot establish; describe what the text
addresses. Do not invent guaranteed learning, business gains or exclusive secrets.
These fields are a planning contract, not public labels or an engagement score.

Resolve scene references from the extracted section IDs and anchors; do not
calculate them from chapter numbers, since part headings and introductions can
shift the sequence. A claim ledger cites substantive supporting prose. A chapter
heading is a useful locator, but it is not evidence for the claims beneath it.

## Move deliberately between three lenses

Mark each scene and spoken beat as `book`, `content` or `reader` in working data.

**Book:** why this text exists, what argument it develops, how its parts connect,
what makes its perspective distinctive, and what kind of book it is.

**Content:** a short, substantive encounter with one idea, mechanism, conflict or
example. Attribute proposals and interpretations. Give the viewer something real.

**Reader:** why that idea matters for the prospective reader, what reading helps
them understand or question, and where the text develops it further.

Signal transitions naturally: “The book begins with…”, a concrete situation,
then “That distinction gives you a way to judge…”. Do not recite “the book says”
in every sentence or announce the lens names on screen. A book can be a recurring
visual anchor in book/reader passages; enter its illustrated world for samples.

## Balance substance and curiosity

Interpret why/how/what at the level of the book and the reading experience:
why this book deserves attention, how it develops its distinctive perspective,
and what the reader will encounter or gain. The subject's mechanism supports
that invitation; it must not consume the film by default.

Give the book's identity, reason to exist and reader value within the opening
30–45 seconds, with a specific hook earlier. Select a few substantive ideas as
evidence of the reading promise. After a sample, return to its role in the book
and its value to the reader. An example should not become the film's main plot
unless the book itself and the brief justify that choice.

Explain a meaningful distinction fully enough to reward watching; preserve the
larger development for reading. Do not substitute a catalog of features, a table
of contents, or an exhaustive walkthrough. Do not withhold every answer behind
empty questions. Curiosity comes from seeing a useful perspective and wanting
its depth, not from manufactured suspense or inflated marketing language.

Reveal central nonfiction ideas when the brief permits. Reserve fuller reasoning,
comparisons, cases and practical detail rather than pretending basic definitions
are spoilers. Respect stricter fiction/spoiler requirements when applicable.
Never imply that a question is answered in the book if it remains unresolved.

End with a concrete reading invitation: identify what merits further attention
and where to begin. “Read the book to learn more” alone is insufficient. Keep
navigation within the established player/page architecture.

## Review the script before rendering voice

Answer these questions from the actual script, citing scene/beat IDs:

1. Why does this particular book exist?
2. What is its central message or perspective?
3. What does it contain, beyond a list of chapter titles?
4. What can a suitable reader understand better after reading?
5. Which content sample makes that promise credible?
6. What worthwhile depth remains in the book, and where?
7. Does the ending invite reading through a specific reason and starting point?

Also review whether the film sounds like a product pitch or a tutorial with
“the book” pasted onto the sentences. If viewers could mistake it for a demo
of the subject, rework its editorial purpose, selection and visual framing.
A structurally valid plan cannot establish reader curiosity; report actual
review scope and distinguish it from audience feedback.
